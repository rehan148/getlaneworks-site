How it's worked out

  • Volumetric weight = CBM × the mode's kg-per-cbm factor (air 167, express 200, road 250 or 333).
  • Chargeable weight = the greater of actual and volumetric weight, rounded up to the next half kilo here. Carriers' rounding rules vary.
  • LCL is charged per revenue tonne: the greater of CBM and tonnes.

Questions

What is chargeable weight?

Chargeable weight is the weight a carrier bills on: the greater of the actual gross weight and the volumetric (dimensional) weight. It stops light, bulky cargo from travelling for the price of its weight alone.

How is volumetric weight calculated for air freight?

Length × width × height in centimetres, divided by 6,000, for each piece. That works out at 167 kg per cubic metre. Courier and express services usually divide by 5,000 (200 kg per cubic metre).

What conversion factor does road freight use?

It varies by carrier and country. Australian road carriers commonly use a cubic conversion of 250 kg per cubic metre; many European groupage carriers use 333 kg per cubic metre. Always use the factor in your carrier's rate card.

Why does density matter?

Density (kg per cubic metre) tells you at a glance which side wins. Below about 167 kg/cbm, air freight is charged on volume; above 1,000 kg/cbm, LCL is charged on weight.
