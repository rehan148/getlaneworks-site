How to calculate chargeable weight

  • Volumetric weight = CBM × the mode's kg-per-cbm factor (air 167, express 200, road 250 or 333).
  • Chargeable weight = the greater of actual and volumetric weight, rounded up to the next half kilo here. Carriers' rounding rules vary.
  • LCL is charged per revenue tonne: the greater of CBM and tonnes.

Worked example (air freight): 10 cartons of 60 × 50 × 40 cm at 15 kg each. Total volume is 1.2 cbm and the actual weight is 150 kg. Volumetric weight at 1:6,000 is 60 × 50 × 40 ÷ 6,000 = 20 kg per carton, 200 kg in total. 200 kg is more than 150 kg, so the airline charges on 200 kg. The same cartons by express (1:5,000) charge on 240 kg.

Air freight chargeable weight: pallets and cartons on one air waybill

Most real air shipments mix sizes. Work out the volume of every piece, add it up, and compare the total volumetric weight with the total actual weight. The greater one is what the airline charges.

PiecesSize (cm)VolumeVolumetric (÷ 6,000)Actual
3 pallets120 × 100 × 1103.96 cbm660 kg540 kg
4 cartons50 × 40 × 300.24 cbm40 kg48 kg
Total4.2 cbm700 kg588 kg

The shipment charges on 700 kg. Its density is 140 kg per cbm, below the 167 kg per cbm break-even for air, so volume wins. At a sample rate of A$6.20 per kg with a A$250 minimum, the freight is 700 × 6.20 = A$4,340.00. A quote built on the actual 588 kg would say A$3,645.60, and the A$694.40 difference comes out of your margin when the airline bills on 700 kg.

Note the cartons on their own would charge on weight (48 kg against 40 kg). It is the total that counts, not each piece.

Road and LCL: chargeable weight in kg per cbm

Road and sea freight use a kg-per-cbm factor instead of a divisor. Multiply the cubic metres by the factor and compare with the actual weight.

Road example: 5 pallets of 120 × 100 × 140 cm at 210 kg each. That is 8.4 cbm and 1,050 kg actual. At 250 kg per cbm, common for Australian road carriers, the cubic weight is 8.4 × 250 = 2,100 kg, double the actual weight. At 333 kg per cbm, common in European groupage, it is 2,797.5 kg after rounding up to the next half kilo. Tall, light pallets cost far more by road than their weight suggests.

LCL example: sea freight LCL uses 1,000 kg per cbm, so each cubic metre or each tonne is one revenue tonne (W/M), whichever is greater. At a sample rate of A$85 per W/M with a 1 W/M minimum:

CBM to chargeable weight

To convert CBM to chargeable weight, multiply the cubic metres by the mode's factor to get volumetric weight, then charge on whichever is greater: volumetric or actual weight.

ModeFactor1 cbm equals
Air (1:6,000)167 kg per cbm166.7 kg
Express and courier (1:5,000)200 kg per cbm200 kg
Road, Australia (common)250 kg per cbm250 kg
Road, European groupage (common)333 kg per cbm333 kg
LCL sea freight1 revenue tonne1 cbm or 1,000 kg, whichever is greater

Example: a 2.4 cbm shipment weighing 300 kg. By air it charges on 400 kg (2.4 × 1,000,000 ÷ 6,000). By express, 480 kg. By road at 250 kg per cbm, 600 kg; at 333, 799.5 kg after rounding up to the next half kilo. By LCL it charges on 2.4 revenue tonnes, because the volume is greater than 0.3 tonnes.

Chargeable weight in inches and pounds

If the dimensions are in inches, you do not need to convert to centimetres first. One cubic inch is 16.387064 cubic centimetres, so the air freight divisor of 6,000 cm³ per kg becomes about 366 cubic inches per kg, or about 166 cubic inches per pound. Express at 5,000 cm³ per kg becomes about 305 cubic inches per kg, or about 138 per pound.

ModeMetric divisorInches to kgInches to lb
Air (1:6,000)6,000 cm³ per kg÷ 366÷ 166
Express and courier (1:5,000)5,000 cm³ per kg÷ 305÷ 138

Worked example (inches): 10 cartons of 24 × 20 × 18 inches at 22 kg each. Each carton is 8,640 cubic inches, 1.416 cbm in total. Volumetric weight by air is 8,640 ÷ 366 = 23.6 kg per carton, 236 kg in total. The actual weight is 220 kg, so the airline charges on 236 kg. In pounds: 8,640 ÷ 166 = 52.0 lb per carton, about 520 lb in total, against an actual 485 lb.

Conversions computed by code from 1 inch = 2.54 cm and 1 kg = 2.20462 lb. The calculator above takes centimetres; the CBM calculator takes inches.

Questions

What is chargeable weight?

Chargeable weight is the weight a carrier bills on: the greater of the actual gross weight and the volumetric (dimensional) weight. It stops light, bulky cargo from travelling for the price of its weight alone.

How is volumetric weight calculated for air freight?

Length × width × height in centimetres, divided by 6,000, for each piece. That works out at 167 kg per cubic metre. Courier and express services usually divide by 5,000 (200 kg per cubic metre).

What conversion factor does road freight use?

It varies by carrier and country. Australian road carriers commonly use a cubic conversion of 250 kg per cubic metre; many European groupage carriers use 333 kg per cubic metre. Always use the factor in your carrier's rate card.

Why does density matter?

Density (kg per cubic metre) tells you at a glance which side wins. Below about 167 kg/cbm, air freight is charged on volume; above 1,000 kg/cbm, LCL is charged on weight.
