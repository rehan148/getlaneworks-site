Questions

What is the difference between demurrage and detention?

Demurrage is charged for a full container that stays in the port or terminal beyond the free time. Detention is charged for keeping the carrier's container outside the terminal (after gate-out) beyond its free time, until the empty is returned. Some carriers combine them into one free-time period.

How are demurrage days counted?

Usually free time starts on the day of discharge (or gate-out for detention) and every calendar day counts, weekends and holidays included, but carriers' tariffs differ. Use the counting option above to match your carrier.

Why are demurrage rates tiered?

Carriers raise the daily rate the longer a container is kept, to get equipment back into circulation. A typical tariff has a lower rate for the first few days after free time and a higher rate after that.

How do forwarders avoid demurrage for customers?

Book the delivery and customs clearance before the ship arrives, negotiate extra free days into the rate, and warn the customer of the last free day in the arrival notice. Including the free days in every quote avoids disputes later.
