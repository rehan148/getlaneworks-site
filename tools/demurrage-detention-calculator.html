How to calculate demurrage charges

Demurrage charges are worked out in five steps. The example below is an illustration with sample rates, not any carrier's tariff, and every figure is computed by code.

Example: 3 × 40' containers are discharged on Monday 2 March and picked up (gate-out) on Friday 13 March. The tariff gives 5 free days, then US$90 per container per day for the first 4 days over free time and US$180 per day after that. The discharge day counts as day 1.

  1. Count the days used. 2 March to 13 March is 12 days, counting the discharge day as day 1.
  2. Find the last free day. With 5 free days the last free day is Friday 6 March. Put this date on the arrival notice.
  3. Take off the free days. 12 days less 5 free days leaves 7 chargeable days.
  4. Split the chargeable days into the tiers. 4 days at US$90 = US$360, then 3 days at US$180 = US$540.
  5. Add up and multiply by the containers. US$900 per container, US$2,700 for the 3 containers.

If the tariff starts counting the day after discharge, the same dates give 11 days, 6 chargeable, and US$720 per container (US$2,160 for 3). Enter the same figures in the calculator above to check them, and match the counting option to the tariff, including how it treats weekends and public holidays.

Calculation of demurrage vs detention

The calculation of demurrage and of detention is the same arithmetic: days used, less free days, priced through the tiers. What changes is where the container is and when the clock runs.

  • Demurrage: the full container is still inside the terminal after its free time. The clock starts at discharge and stops at gate-out.
  • Detention: the container is outside the terminal and kept past its free time. The clock starts at gate-out and stops when the empty is returned (on exports, from empty pick-up to return of the full box to the terminal).
  • Combined free time: some carriers combine the two into one free time from discharge to empty return, with one set of tiers. The separate or combined free time table further down shows how far apart the two methods can land on the same dates.
  • Terminal storage: in some ports the terminal also charges its own storage for a box left in the yard, separate from the carrier's demurrage.

Detention charges calculation: a worked example

Detention is calculated the same way, starting from gate-out instead of discharge. Sample figures again, not any carrier's tariff: 3 containers leave the terminal on 2 December and the empties are returned on 20 December. The tariff gives 7 free days, then 60 a day for 4 days, 120 a day for the next 5 days and 200 a day after that.

  1. Days used: 2 to 20 December is 19 days when the gate-out day counts as day 1.
  2. Chargeable days: 19 less 7 free days leaves 12.
  3. Tiers: 4 × 60 = 240, 5 × 120 = 600, 3 × 200 = 600.
  4. Total: 1,440 per container, 4,320 for 3 containers.

If the carrier starts counting the day after gate-out, the same dates give 18 days, 11 chargeable, and 1,240 per container (3,720 for 3). One counting rule moves the bill by 600, so read the tariff before you quote the free time.

Demurrage & detention calculator: separate or combined free time

Some carriers give separate free time for demurrage (in the terminal) and detention (outside it). Others give one combined free time from discharge to empty return. The same dates can produce very different bills, so check which one the tariff uses before you quote. Sample figures, not any carrier's tariff, computed by code. US demurrage and detention billing rules

For US trades, the Federal Maritime Commission's billing rule (46 CFR Part 541, published 23 February 2024, most of it in effect from 28 May 2024) sets who can be billed and when:

  • An invoice can go only to the party that contracted for the transport or storage, or to the consignee, and not to several parties at once.
  • Ocean carriers and terminal operators must issue the invoice within 30 calendar days of when the charges were last incurred. An NVOCC must pass it on within 30 calendar days of the invoice it received.
  • The billed party has at least 30 calendar days to ask for a reduction, refund or waiver, and the billing party must try to resolve a timely request within 30 calendar days.
  • The rule also lists information an invoice must contain. The FMC said that section (541.6) would take effect once separately approved, so check its current status with the FMC.

Source: FMC announcement, 23 February 2024, checked 20 November 2026. This is a summary, not legal advice.

Put free time in every quote

A quote that never says how many free days are included leaves room for a dispute when the demurrage bill arrives. Show the free time and the daily rates after it on the quote itself, next to the Incoterm, so the customer knows the last free day before the ship arrives. Questions

How do you calculate demurrage charges?

Count the days from discharge to gate-out, take off the free days, split the chargeable days into the carrier's tiers, multiply each tier by its daily rate and add up, then multiply by the number of containers. For example, with sample rates: 12 days used, 5 free, 7 chargeable; 4 days at US$90 and 3 days at US$180 is US$900 per container.

What is the difference between demurrage and detention?

Demurrage is charged for a full container that stays in the port or terminal beyond the free time. Detention is charged for keeping the carrier's container outside the terminal (after gate-out) beyond its free time, until the empty is returned. Some carriers combine them into one free-time period.

How are demurrage days counted?

Usually free time starts on the day of discharge (or gate-out for detention) and every calendar day counts, weekends and holidays included, but carriers' tariffs differ. Use the counting option above to match your carrier.

Why are demurrage rates tiered?

Carriers raise the daily rate the longer a container is kept, to get equipment back into circulation. A typical tariff has a lower rate for the first few days after free time and a higher rate after that.

How do forwarders avoid demurrage for customers?

Book the delivery and customs clearance before the ship arrives, negotiate extra free days into the rate, and warn the customer of the last free day in the arrival notice. Including the free days in every quote avoids disputes later.

Is demurrage charged per container?

Yes. Container demurrage and detention are charged per container per day, so the daily rate is multiplied by the number of containers. Rates usually differ by container type, such as 20', 40' and reefer.
