LCL vs FCL: when to quote which, and how to show both

5 November 2026 · All articles

In short: LCL is usually cheaper below about 12–15 cbm and FCL above it, but the crossover depends on CFS charges, minimums and the lane. When it's close, quote both and let the customer choose.

"Should this go LCL or FCL?" is one of the most common questions a forwarder gets, and one of the easiest to get wrong by looking only at the ocean rate. The right answer depends on volume, the destination CFS charges, how fragile and time-sensitive the cargo is, and whether the customer can receive a container. Here's how to decide, and how to quote so the customer sees the trade-off.

How each one is charged

  • LCL is charged per revenue tonne (W/M), the greater of cubic metres and tonnes, with a minimum, plus origin and destination CFS charges that are also per W/M or per shipment. See how to calculate an LCL quote.
  • FCL is a flat rate per container plus terminal handling, documentation and delivery per container, whatever is inside. A 20' holds about 28–33 cbm of usable space, a 40' about 58–67, a 40HC about 68–76.

The break-even volume

Because FCL is flat and LCL grows with every cubic metre, there's a volume above which the container is cheaper even half empty. On many Asia to Australia lanes that crossover sits somewhere around 12 to 15 cbm against a 20', but it moves with the lane: higher CFS charges or a high LCL rate pull it down, cheap container rates pull it down too, and a low LCL rate pushes it up. Work it out from your own rate sheet rather than a rule of thumb: price the shipment both ways and compare the all-in totals.

Worked example (sample rates)

14 cbm, 3,200 kg of general cargo, FOB Ningbo to Melbourne, door delivery in the Melbourne metro, clearance required.

LCL (14 W/M)
  Ocean freight 14 x 85 .............................. AUD 1,190
  Destination CFS & docs .............................. AUD 320
  Destination CFS handling 14 x 45 .................... AUD 630
  Customs clearance & BICON lodgement ................. AUD 180
  Door delivery (metro, LCL) .......................... AUD 180
  Total ............................................... AUD 2,500

FCL (1 x 20GP)
  Ocean freight ....................................... AUD 1,850
  Destination terminal & docs ......................... AUD 780
  Customs clearance & BICON lodgement ................. AUD 180
  Door delivery (metro, 20') .......................... AUD 420
  Total ............................................... AUD 3,230

At 14 cbm LCL still wins by about AUD 730 on these sample rates. Re-run it at 20 cbm: LCL rises by 6 x (85 + 45) = AUD 780 to about 3,280, and the container is now cheaper. The crossover on this lane is just under 20 cbm, not 12.

What the ocean rate doesn't show

  • Transit and handling. LCL is consolidated and deconsolidated, so it usually takes longer and is handled more. Fragile or high-value cargo often justifies FCL below the break-even.
  • Delivery. FCL door delivery needs somewhere a truck can drop or unpack a container; LCL arrives on pallets. A customer without a dock may prefer LCL even when FCL is cheaper.
  • Minimums. Small LCL shipments pay the 1 W/M minimum plus fixed CFS fees, so very small consignments can cost more per cubic metre than you'd expect.
  • Peak season. LCL space is usually easier to find when containers are tight, and vice versa.

How to quote it

When the volume is near the crossover, quote both options in the same reply, each as a complete all-in total with the same lines, and say in one sentence which you'd recommend and why ("FCL is AUD 300 more but arrives a week sooner and isn't unpacked at the CFS"). Customers trust a forwarder who shows the trade-off. Keep the layout consistent with your quote reply template, and state validity for both.
