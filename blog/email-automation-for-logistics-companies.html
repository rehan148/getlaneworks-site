Email automation for logistics companies: what to automate and what to keep

24 November 2026 · By the Laneworks team · All articles

In short: Email automation for logistics companies works in three layers. Inbox rules sort mail by sender and subject. Templates and reminders cut typing. AI reading turns a customer's free-text email into clean fields so software can do the work behind it, such as pricing a quote from your rate sheet. Automate the repeat emails first, keep prices on fixed rules, and keep a person on every exception.

Email automation for logistics companies means software handling the repeat emails that fill a freight, forwarding or 3PL inbox: quote requests, booking confirmations, status questions, document chasers and rate updates. Most logistics work still starts and ends in email. Customers ask for prices by email, agents send rates by email, and carriers send notices by email. This guide covers which of those emails can be automated, which tools fit each one, a worked quote example, and where a person should stay in the loop.

The three layers of email automation

  • Inbox rules. Outlook rules and Gmail filters act on mail by sender, subject or words in the message: move it, flag it, forward it, label it. See Microsoft's guide to rules in Outlook and Google's guide to filters in Gmail. Rules sort mail. They do not understand it.
  • Templates, sequences and reminders. Saved replies, shared inbox assignment and follow-up reminders. They cut typing and stop emails being missed, but a person still reads every message and does the work.
  • AI reading plus fixed rules. A language model reads the email the way a person would and pulls out the facts: origin, destination, cargo, weights, Incoterm, dates. Fixed rules then do the work from your own data. This is the layer that removes work rather than moving it around.

Most logistics teams already use the first two layers. The time sink is the third: emails where someone has to read, look something up, calculate and write back.

Start with the email that repeats most and decides revenue. For a freight forwarder that is almost always the quote request: it arrives every day, it takes real lookup work, and a slow reply can lose the job. More on that in how to answer freight quote requests faster.

How to automate customer emails in logistics, step by step

  1. Give each email type one address. A shared address for quotes, another for bookings, another for accounts. Automation is far easier when one inbox carries one kind of work.
  2. Sort with rules first. Route by sender and subject so the right team sees the right mail. This costs nothing and is already in Outlook and Gmail.
  3. Get your data into one place. Software can only answer from data it can read. For quotes that means one rate sheet with lanes, equipment, validity dates and local charges. See how to structure a freight rate sheet.
  4. Let AI read, and only read. Use it to turn the email into fields and to spot what is missing (no weight, no Incoterm, no delivery postcode). Never let it decide a price.
  5. Do the work on fixed rules. Same input, same output. Prices, totals and dates come from your tables, so every figure can be traced back to a line on your sheet.
  6. Check before sending, then switch on. Have your team check drafts on real work. Once the results hold up on your own requests, let fully priced replies go out automatically and keep the exceptions with a person.

Inbox tools vs automation that does the work

Shared inbox tools assign emails, track who replied and add templates. They make a busy inbox easier to manage, and many logistics teams run on one. But they leave the work itself with your team: someone still opens the rate sheet, works out the chargeable weight, adds the local charges and types the quote.

Automation that does the work reads the email, carries out the task from your own data, and hands your team a finished draft to check. The question to ask any vendor is simple: after the email arrives, what does my team still have to do by hand? A worked example: one quote request email

A customer writes: "Hi, can you quote 1 x 40HC FOB Shanghai to our warehouse in Dandenong South VIC 3175? General cargo, ready 8 Dec. Need customs clearance too."

The AI reads it into fields: 1 x 40HC, FOB Shanghai, door delivery to Dandenong South 3175, general cargo, ready 8 December, customs clearance required. Under FOB the buyer pays the main freight and destination charges, so origin charges are left off (see Incoterms for quoting). Fixed rules then price each line from the rate sheet and add the forwarder's markup, here 20%. The figures below use sample rates, computed by code:

Now a second email asks for a 40ft reefer to a lane that is not on the sheet. Nothing is guessed. The request is flagged for a person, and the rate request to the overseas agent is written with the cargo, temperature, dates and Incoterm already filled in. What should stay with a person

  • Prices that are not on your sheet. New lanes, expired rates, reefer, dangerous goods, oversize and RORO need a partner rate and judgement.
  • Complaints, claims and delays. These need someone who can make a call and own the relationship.
  • Anything that commits money. Credit terms, refunds to customers and payment changes should never go out on their own.
  • The first weeks of any automation. Check drafts on your own real work before any reply goes out automatically.

Questions

What is email automation for logistics companies?

Software that handles the repeat emails in a logistics inbox: sorting mail, sending templates and reminders, and reading customer emails so the work behind them, such as pricing a quote, is done from your own data.

Can Outlook or Gmail rules automate logistics emails?

Partly. Rules and filters can move, label, flag and forward mail by sender, subject or words. They cannot read a quote request and work out a price. For that you need software that reads the email and applies your rates.

Is it safe to let AI reply to customer emails?

It is safe when the AI only reads and drafts, every number comes from a table you control, anything outside your rules goes to a person, and your team checks drafts on real work before any reply is sent automatically.

Which logistics emails should be automated first?

The one that repeats most and decides revenue. For freight forwarders that is the customer quote request, followed by rate requests to partners and follow-ups on unanswered quotes.

Do I need to change my TMS or CargoWise to automate emails?

No. Email automation that works from your inbox and your existing rate sheet runs alongside CargoWise, Magaya, WiseGrid or a spreadsheet.
