How to get instant freight quotes: what to send, and how forwarders reply fast

22 November 2026 · By the Laneworks team · All articles

In short: A quote comes back fast when the request has everything needed to price it. Send the six core details in one email. Forwarders reply fast when every price sits in one rate sheet and the reply is assembled from it, not typed from scratch.

How to get instant freight quotes comes down to two things: a complete request from the customer, and a forwarder who can price it without a round of questions. This guide covers both sides: what to send, what online "instant" quotes usually leave out, and how a forwarder can answer in minutes from its own rate sheet.

What to send to get an instant freight quote

Most delays start with a missing detail. Put these six in the first email:

  1. Origin and destination: the port or airport, or the full pickup and delivery address with postcode if you need door service.
  2. Incoterm: EXW, FOB, CIF, DAP and so on. It decides which charges the forwarder should quote. See Incoterms for quoting.
  3. Cargo: what it is, and whether any of it is dangerous goods (class and UN number if yes).
  4. Weights: gross weight per piece and in total.
  5. Dimensions: length, width and height per carton or pallet, and the number of pieces. For sea freight, the total CBM; the CBM calculator works it out in centimetres or inches. For air, dimensions decide the chargeable weight.
  6. Ready date: when the cargo can be collected. Rates are valid for a sailing or flight date, so this decides which rate applies.

Customs clearance, insurance and delivery conditions (forklift on site, tailgate needed) help too. The full list is in the freight quote request checklist.

A request that can be priced straight away

Subject: Quote request: FOB Ningbo to Melbourne, LCL

Origin: Ningbo port (supplier delivers to CFS, FOB)
Destination: door delivery, Dandenong South VIC 3175
Cargo: plastic homewares, not dangerous goods
Pieces: 10 cartons, 24 x 20 x 18 inches each
Weight: 22 kg per carton, 220 kg total
Volume: 1.416 cbm
Ready date: 2 December
Customs clearance: yes. Insurance: no.

Why instant online quotes often leave out local charges

Online instant quotes are built from the details a form can capture. Charges that depend on what happens at destination usually sit outside the price. Flexport's help centre, for example, says customs costs and cargo insurance are not on its quotes, and that storage, demurrage, pre-pull and trucking wait fees may be invoiced depending on circumstances at destination.

So before comparing two instant quotes, check what each one includes: origin charges, destination terminal and handling charges, customs clearance, duty and tax, and delivery. A cheaper number that leaves out destination charges is not cheaper. A forwarder quoting from its own rate sheet can show every one of those lines, with the exclusions written out.

How a forwarder can reply fast from its own rate sheet

For forwarders, an instant quote is mostly an organisation problem. The steps that speed it up:

  • Keep every price in one rate sheet: freight by lane and equipment, local charges by Incoterm, door delivery by postcode zone, clearance. See how to structure a freight rate sheet.
  • Price on stated assumptions: if the Incoterm or delivery conditions are missing, price the most common case, mark the assumption and ask in the same reply.
  • Use one reply format: the same order of lines, totals, validity and exclusions every time. The freight quote email template is a starting point.
  • Flag what the sheet doesn't cover: a new lane, reefer, DG or oversize needs a partner rate. Send that rate request at once instead of guessing.
  • Measure the response time: the first complete reply often wins the job. See why quote response time decides who wins.
