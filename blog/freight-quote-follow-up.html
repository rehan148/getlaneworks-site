Following up freight quotes: how to win more of the ones you send

5 November 2026 · By the Laneworks team · All articles

In short: When to follow up, what to say, a follow-up template, asking why you lost, and tracking your quote win rate.

Small forwarders spend a lot of effort on the quote and almost none on what happens after it's sent. Many quotes are never answered: the customer went with whoever replied first, the shipment moved, or your quote sank in their inbox. A short, well-timed follow-up wins back some of those jobs for a few minutes' work. This article covers when to follow up, what to say and how to tell whether it's working.

Speed comes first

The best follow-up is a fast first reply. A customer asking three forwarders for a price often books with the first one that gives a complete, clear quote. If your replies take most of a day, follow-ups are fixing a problem that starts earlier. Our guide to getting quote response time under an hour covers that side.

When to follow up

  • Same day, for urgent or air shipments. If the customer said "urgent" or the cargo is ready now, a check-in a few hours later is reasonable.
  • Two working days, for most sea freight. Long enough for them to compare quotes, short enough that they haven't booked yet.
  • Before the quote expires. A reminder a day or two before validity ends gives a natural reason to write.
  • Once more after that, then stop. Two follow-ups is usually enough. After that, note it and move on.

What to say

A follow-up works when it makes the next step easy. Don't just ask "any update?". Give them something to act on: a question that unblocks the booking, a change that affects the price, or an offer to adjust the quote.

Hi [name],

Just checking you got the quote for the [pieces/container] from [origin] to [destination].

If the cargo-ready date or volume has changed, send me the new details and I'll update it today. If you'd like to go ahead, reply "book it" and I'll confirm space.

The rate is valid until [date].

[Your name]

Find out why you lost

When a customer says no, ask one question: was it price, transit time, or something else? Most will answer in a line. After a few weeks of answers you'll know whether you're losing on rates, on speed or on clarity, and each needs a different fix. If it's price on certain lanes, look at your buy rates and how you set margins. If it's speed, look at how long quotes take to go out.

Track your win rate

Keep a simple log: date requested, date quoted, customer, lane, amount, followed up (yes/no) and outcome. Your win rate is quotes won divided by quotes sent. Two numbers in that log tell you most of what you need: the win rate on quotes you followed up versus those you didn't, and the win rate on quotes sent within an hour versus later. Even a spreadsheet kept for a month will show you where the jobs are going.
