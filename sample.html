What a draft looks like

A customer sent this quote request to a forwarder. The forwarder forwarded it to their Laneworks address. About two minutes later, this landed in the ops inbox. Every number comes from the forwarder's own rate sheet (here, a sample tariff); nothing is guessed.

Examples: Australia (AUD) · United States (USD)

1. The customer's email

From: Daniel Ho <daniel@harbourline-example.com.au> Subject: Quote request - 40HC Ningbo Hi team, Can you please quote 1 x 40HC FOB Ningbo to Melbourne, door delivery to Dandenong South 3175. Cargo is outdoor furniture, approx 9,800 kg / 62 cbm. Cargo ready 14 Nov. We'll need customs clearance too. Thanks, Daniel Harbourline Homewares (example)

Three parts: the reply to paste and send, what was read from the email (so you can check it in seconds), and the price breakdown with the rate-sheet row behind every line.

The same request into the US

Same customer email, sent to a US forwarder whose rate sheet is in USD. The draft adds the US lines a complete quote needs: entry and ISF filing, pier pass for Los Angeles/Long Beach, and trucking to the ZIP code. Mixed-currency sheets (USD freight with CAD or GBP local charges) show a total for each currency.
