What it shows

  • Prices come from the sheet, not the model. The AI reads the email; fixed rules set every number, so every total above can be rebuilt from the rate sheet with a calculator.
  • Gaps are flagged, never filled. A reefer, dangerous goods, or a lane the sheet doesn't cover gets a note to your team, not an invented rate.
  • The freight details are read properly. Air chargeable weight, LCL weight-or-measure, 20' vs 40', destination-only quotes on CIF arrivals, door delivery by postcode, clearance when asked.
  • Noise is ignored. Old quotes in a reply chain, addresses in a signature, newsletters and status chasers don't turn into quotes.

How each price is set

Port pair and container size from the FCL sheet; W/M (the greater of cbm and tonnes) for LCL; chargeable weight (actual or volumetric at the sheet's factor) for air; local charges by side and Incoterm; door delivery by postcode band; clearance when the customer asks. Questions

Were these requests written to make Laneworks look good?

They were written to cover what goes wrong on a real desk: codes, typos, reply chains, attachments, cargo the sheet can't price. Download the set and judge for yourself, or send your own requests and we'll run those instead.

Why are some results counted as correct without a price?

Because the right answer was not to price. A reefer or dangerous-goods request priced off a dry general-cargo sheet would be a wrong quote; flagging it for a real rate is the correct outcome.

Does the AI ever set a price?

No. The AI reads the email and pulls out the shipment details. Every amount comes from the rate sheet by fixed rules, and each line on the draft names the sheet row it came from.
