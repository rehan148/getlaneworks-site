Why the AI never sets the price

Language models are good at reading messy emails and bad at arithmetic you can audit. So Laneworks splits the job. AI reads the lane, cargo, weights, Incoterm and delivery address. Fixed rules price it from your sheet. Every line names the row it came from. Anything not on the sheet, or past its validity date, is flagged and never guessed.

Drafts first, or replies that send themselves

You choose how much the agent does alone. Start with drafts: every priced reply comes to your team to check and send. Switch on automatic replies and a request that is fully priced from your rates, with nothing flagged, goes straight back to the customer from your own mailbox, nights and weekends included. A missing rate, a guessed detail or a quote above the limit you set still comes to your team as a draft, and you get a copy of every reply sent.

How to judge any AI agent for freight forwarding

  • Where do the prices come from? Your rates, the vendor's rate database, or the model's own guess. Ask to see one line traced back to its source.
  • What happens when a rate is missing? A good agent stops and says which rate it needs. A bad one fills the gap.
  • What stops it sending? There should be clear rules: fully priced only, nothing flagged, under a value you set.
  • Do you have to move systems? Some agents need your rates loaded into their platform first. Laneworks works from the rate sheet you already keep, next to CargoWise, Magaya or a spreadsheet.
  • Is the price published? If you can't see it, you can't work out the payback before the first call.

Questions

What is an AI agent for freight forwarders?

Software that takes one back-office job from start to finish with little or no typing from your team. For quoting, that means reading the customer's request, pricing it, writing the reply and sending it, or handing it to a person when something is missing.

Is Laneworks a chatbot?

No. It works in email, where quote requests already arrive. Your customers don't log in to anything. Your team gets drafts by email and can open each quote in Laneworks Desk.

What does it read?

The email body and attached PDF, Excel, CSV and Word files such as packing lists and commercial invoices. Phone requests can be typed into the quote pad while you are on the call.

Which freight?

FCL, LCL, air and road, including container cartage. Origin and destination charges by Incoterm, door delivery by postcode zone and customs clearance, all from your own rates.

Does it replace my team?

No. It takes the typing and the rate lookups off your team. Anything unusual still goes to a person, and your team decides which quotes may send on their own.
