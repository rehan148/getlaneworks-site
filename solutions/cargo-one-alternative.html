When cargo.one is the better fit

If most of your work is air freight and you want airline rates, capacity and booking in one platform, cargo.one covers more ground. It also offers a branded customer portal and booking agents. Laneworks doesn't book capacity.

When Laneworks is the better fit

  • Your best rates are your own. Contract rates, agent rates, cartage, clearance and your margin. Laneworks prices from exactly those and shows the row behind every line.
  • You don't want another platform. Requests are forwarded from your inbox and replies go out from it. It runs next to CargoWise, Magaya or a spreadsheet.
  • Road and door-to-door matter. Pallet, container cartage and door delivery by postcode zone, from your own road rates.
  • You want the price up front. Plans are on the pricing page, with unlimited users and no per-quote fees.

Questions

What is the main difference between cargo.one and Laneworks?

cargo.one is a quoting and booking platform that combines its own air and ocean rate database with your rates, with AI agents for quoting, rate procurement and bookings. Laneworks does one job: it prices every quote request from the rate sheet you already keep, by fixed rules, and replies in your name from email.

Can Laneworks book airline capacity?

No. Laneworks stops at the quote. cargo.one offers air e-booking with 75+ airlines on its own site.

Where does each price on a Laneworks quote come from?

From your own rate sheet. Every line names the row it came from. Anything not on the sheet, or past its validity date, is flagged for your team and never guessed.

Does Laneworks publish its pricing?

Yes. Plans start at A$1,500 a month for up to 150 quote requests, with unlimited users and no per-quote fees. cargo.one's pricing page does not list prices.

Is Laneworks affiliated with cargo.one?

No. cargo.one is a trademark of its owner. This page compares information cargo.one publishes on cargo.one, checked November 2026.
