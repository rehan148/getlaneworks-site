Three pricing models, and who they suit

  • Per user (Quotiss, Logistaas): cheap for one or two people, grows with every hire.
  • Flat plan with seats (Linbis): predictable, but you move your operations into a new system.
  • Flat per mailbox (Laneworks): the whole team forwards to one address, so the price doesn't grow with headcount or with quote volume.
  • Usage or custom quote (CargoWise, GoFreight, Magaya): you only learn the price after a sales call.

Which one should you buy?

  • You need shipments, documents, warehousing and accounting in one place, and you're small: look at Linbis or Logistaas. They're cheaper than Laneworks per month and do far more than quoting.
  • You want a better form to build quotes in: look at Quotiss.
  • Your system is fine but the quote inbox is the bottleneck, and slow replies are losing you jobs: that's what Laneworks is for. Nobody types the quote; it's written from your rates and sent in about two minutes.

How it works

  • Forward the customer's quote request to your Laneworks address, attachments and all.
  • We read it: lane, cargo, weights and dimensions, Incoterm and delivery address are pulled out of the email and any packing list.
  • We price it by fixed rules against your own rate sheet. The AI never sets a price, and every line names the rate-sheet row it came from.
  • Sent for you, or checked by you. With auto-reply on, fully priced quotes go straight to your customer from your own mailbox, usually within two minutes, 24/7. Anything unusual comes to your team as a draft.
